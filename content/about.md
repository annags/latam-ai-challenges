## About this repository


This repository provides an interactive overview of AI governance challenges in Latin America, built from a systematic literature review of 38 peer-reviewed sources (2021–2026). It is part of Study 1 of ongoing doctoral research at the Universitat Oberta de Catalunya (UOC).


### Research context


AI adoption is accelerating across Latin America, yet the region accounts for only 1.12% of global AI investment despite representing 8.8% of the world's population and 6.6% of global GDP (ILIA 2025). At the same time, ECLAC (2025) identifies three structural development traps — low growth potential, high inequality and weak social cohesion, and ineffective governance — that are deeply interrelated and shape how the region engages with AI governance.


This study advances a conceptual architecture of AI governance challenges in Latin America, with attention to their thematic content and their regional specificity.


### Research questions


- **RQ1.** What ethical, institutional, and socio-technical challenges are hindering AI governance in Latin America according to the academic 
  literature?
- **RQ2.** How does the regional context shape these challenges, and to 
  what degree do multilateral assessments — such as the ILIA 2025 and 
  UNESCO's Readiness Assessment Methodology — interrogate the picture 
  emerging from the academic literature?
- **RQ3.** How do these regional challenges compare to globally oriented AI governance frameworks?


### Methodology


The review follows the **SALSA framework** (Search, Appraisal, Synthesis, Analysis) as proposed by Codina (2020) and **PRISMA-S** reporting guidelines (Rethlefsen et al., 2021).


Sources were identified across five databases: Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar (global coverage), supplemented by Dialnet and SciELO (regional coverage). The search covered peer-reviewed articles published in English, Spanish, or Portuguese between 2021 and 2026 explicitly addressing AI governance, ethics, or regulation in Latin America.


From 895 records initially identified, 38 articles were included after 
deduplication, title/abstract screening, and quality assessment.


Challenges were coded inductively through qualitative content analysis 
(Mayring, 2014) and organised using a **tripartite framework** adapted 
from Wirtz et al. (2020): socio-technical, ethical, and institutional 
dimensions. Findings are triangulated with the ILIA 2025 (ECLAC–CENIA) 
and UNESCO's AI Readiness Assessment Methodology (RAM).


### Key findings


The review identifies **20 distinct governance challenges** across the 
three dimensions. Several are not theorised in globally oriented 
frameworks (Wirtz et al., 2020) and reflect the region's specific 
historical and political-economic conditions:


- **Data colonialism, extractivism, and technological dependency** 
  emerge as the most structurally distinctive challenges, rooted in 
  Latin America's position in the global political economy.
- **Institutional heterogeneity** is pronounced: governance readiness, research visibility, and multilateral engagement are concentrated in South America's larger economies, while Central America and the Caribbean remain, for now, largely excluded.
- **Two structural gaps** hinder AI governance across the region: the digital divide, particularly pronounced in rural areas, and the gap between ethical principles and enforceable regulatory instruments. 
- **Two governance blind spots** are identified: environmental 
  sustainability (the ecological footprint of AI infrastructure) and 
  child protection in AI-mediated environments.




### How to navigate this repository


- **By challenge category** — Browse the 21 challenges across three 
  analytical dimensions (ethical, institutional, socio-technical), with 
  attention to their regional specificity.
- **By source** — Explore each paper's contribution, including verbatim 
  excerpts illustrating how individual authors frame each challenge.
- **Articles table** — Full bibliographic overview of the 38 reviewed 
  sources.
- **Statistics** — Frequency distribution of challenges, publication 
  trends (2021–2026), and geographical coverage across the region.
